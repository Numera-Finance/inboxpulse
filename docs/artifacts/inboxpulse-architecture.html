The best signal turned out to be counting

A bookkeeper in Pune reads a hundred emails to find the three that matter, and the three rarely say they matter. Two days of measurement on embeddings, ego states, judge panels and synthetic data, and the two things that predict trouble are a complaint rate and a message count.

Every email that survives the spam filter goes to the model. There is no embedding gate in production, so the cost and latency figures below describe what it would buy, not what it saves.

What predicts trouble

One question, asked properly: given what we can see about a client this week, what is the chance they complain next week? None of it needs a model. All of it was in the database the whole time.

Across 9,417 client-weeks, 533 were followed by a complaint. That is the prior — 5.7% — and every claim below is the same probability after conditioning on something we can observe for free.

Evidence only counts when it is independent. Engagement is worth 2.8× alone and a recent complaint 3.0× alone; together they reach 4.4×, because each carries something the other does not. Volume carries nothing new: bolt it onto the pair and 24.7% becomes 25.0% while two thirds of the coverage is thrown away. Acceleration makes it worse, at 23.7%. Loud, fast weeks are mostly engaged weeks, so once engagement is known the volume term is already spent.

A

Being in the conversation is the signal live

Not what the client writes — whether an exchange is happening. Four or more messages from them this week against eight in the prior month, and three or more replies from us.

It also beats the field the fires list used to rank by. Unanswered complaints separate 18.3% from 14.5%; engagement separates 24.7% from 13.0%. Decisively, an engaged client whose complaints have all been answered runs 21.9%, against 14.7% for an unengaged client with complaints still sitting open. Answering the mail does not settle the client. Being in the conversation is what says they are still working themselves up.

So the panel now sorts fires by this first and shows "In conversation" on the rows that carry it. Unanswered stays second, and stays on the row, because it is the part the firm controls and the reason to reply today — it is simply the weaker predictor of what happens next.

engaged → 24.7%
not engaged → 13.0%
counting, no model call
B

Volume, corrected downward live

This was reported here as the strongest early signal in the product, on the strength of "volume rose in 180 of 265 clients — 68%". That number was measured only on clients who eventually complained. It answers "did volume rise before the complaint?" when the question a panel row has to answer is "given volume rose, will they complain?"

Run as a real alert over every client-week instead, the original rule caught 7.5% of complaints at 15.7% precision. What survives is smaller and still worth a slot: among clients with no complaint on file, engaged and above twice their usual volume reaches 13.7% against an 11.0% floor for engaged alone.

Volume without engagement is worse than nothing — 4.4%, below the prior. An unattended spike is a notification stream, not a person getting angrier.

claimed 68%
measured 13.7%
2.4× the prior, not 12×
C

Crossing 10% is a step change that does not revert live

Under 10% in a month, a client behaves exactly like one at zero. Over it, they sit at four times base rate for at least three months and do not drift back.

They do not accelerate — an apparent shrinking of the gap between complaints turned out to be survivorship, and within a single client it is a coin flip. Elevated, not spiralling, which makes an intervention a rescue rather than a race.

under 10% → 2.0% next month
over 10% → 7.9%
still 5.9% three months on

The same mistake twice, one experiment apart. Both the 68% volume figure and an earlier claim that unhappy clients grow more sensitive were produced by selecting on the outcome and then reporting a rate. Conditioning on "they complained" and measuring backwards will make almost any signal look strong at a 5.7% prior. The rule that survives: measure it as the alert it will actually be — fire it on every client-week, then count what followed.

Every model-based approach either failed or restated what was already known. The per-client mood vector stayed flat through a real escalation. Ego state did not move. Clients at their angriest still read as Adult stance — they do not start shouting, they stay polite and get unhappy more often.

04

Gemini decides live

The only paid step and the only one that decides anything. v1.9 learned that asking when about work we already hold is a complaint, after the prompt had explicitly taught the opposite.

This is now the binding constraint. Tightening the gate cannot recover a complaint the judge misreads, because the gate already sent it.

19 of 20 complaints caught
9 false alarms in 49
~$7/month, full coverage
05

Name the device, or say nothing live

Telling someone "this is a complaint" moves one email. Telling them "'not good to have so many iterations' is understatement, read it as 'this is bad'" moves every future email, including the ones we never flag.

Only patterns that name something literally written are shown. Ones that infer what the writer meant were wrong 4 times in 10 and stay silent.

The numbers that matter

Measured against 49 emails a person read and judged, and against 1,015 real complaints. No public corpus matches this domain, so those 49 rows are the only human instrument.

3%of client mail is a complaint
5 of 20complaints that say so plainly
5.7%chance any client complains next week
24.7%if they complained recently and we are in it with them
10%the rate a client never comes back from
5clients escalating right now

Only a quarter of complaints use explicit failure wording. The rest arrive as "could you provide an update on the expected timeline": courteous, blameless, and understood by every American reader as a chase. That gap is the entire product.

The tone branches all died for one reason. Hotspot clients read 94% Adult stance even in their angriest months. They do not start shouting; they stay polite and get unhappy more often. The signal is content and frequency, never register.

What is worth building next

One measured result is not yet in the product, and one instrument beats the model on precision.

IdeaMeasuredState
Promote on a nearest labelled neighbour86% / 60% recallnot built
…the same, on a client with no history75% / 30% recallit is client memory, not detection
Clear mail on benign neighboursloses 6–17 of 20ruled out
Wire the embedding gate7 in 10 at 1-in-5 sentlibrary only

Nearest-neighbour lookup is more precise than the production model — 86% against 70% — but half of that comes from the client's own history. Excluding it drops the result to 75% precision and halves the recall. It is institutional memory rather than complaint detection: it recognises this client complaining again, which is legitimate where the history exists and worth far less on a new tenant.
