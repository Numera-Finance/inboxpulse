The best signal is counting

A bookkeeper in Pune reads a hundred emails to find the three that matter, and the three rarely say they matter. A model finds them one at a time. What says which client is about to blow up is a complaint rate and a message count — no embedding, no inference, already in the database.

Every email that survives the spam filter goes to the model. There is no embedding gate in production, so the cost and latency figures below describe what it would buy, not what it saves.

What predicts trouble

Given what we can see about a client this week, what is the chance they complain next week? None of it needs a model. All of it was in the database the whole time.

Across 9,417 client-weeks, 533 were followed by a complaint. That is the prior — 5.7% — and every claim below is the same probability after conditioning on something we can observe for free.

Evidence only counts when it is independent. Engagement is worth 2.8× alone and a recent complaint 3.0× alone; together they reach 4.4×, because each carries something the other does not. Volume carries nothing new: bolt it onto the pair and 24.7% becomes 25.0% while two thirds of the coverage is thrown away. Acceleration makes it worse, at 23.7%. Loud, fast weeks are mostly engaged weeks, so once engagement is known the volume term is already spent.

A

Being in the conversation is the signal live

Not what the client writes — whether an exchange is happening. Four or more messages from them this week against eight in the prior month, and three or more replies from us.

It outranks the count of unanswered complaints, which separates 18.3% from 14.5%. An engaged client whose complaints have all been answered runs 21.9%, against 14.7% for an unengaged client with complaints still sitting open. Answering the mail does not settle the client. Being in the conversation is what says they are still working themselves up.

The fires list sorts on this first and shows "In conversation" on the rows that carry it. Unanswered sorts second and stays on the row: it is the part the firm controls and the reason to reply today, even though it says less about what happens next.

engaged → 24.7%
not engaged → 13.0%
counting, no model call
B

Volume catches the client nobody has flagged yet live

Every other signal needs a complaint already on file. This one is the only thing in the panel that fires before anyone has written one, which is what earns it a slot at 2.4× when the pair above manage 4.4×.

It works on top of engagement, not instead of it: among clients with nothing on file, engaged and above twice their usual volume reaches 13.7% against an 11.0% floor for engaged alone. Volume with nobody replying runs 4.4%, below the base rate — an unattended spike is a notification stream, not a person getting angrier, and the reply test is what separates them.

engaged + loud → 13.7%
engaged alone → 11.0%
fires with nothing on file
C

Crossing 10% is a step change that does not revert live

Under 10% in a month, a client behaves exactly like one at zero. Over it, they sit at four times base rate for at least three months and do not drift back.

They do not accelerate. Within a single client, the gap between complaints is a coin flip — 24 speeding up against 22 slowing down. Elevated, not spiralling, which makes an intervention a rescue rather than a race.

under 10% → 2.0% next month
over 10% → 7.9%
still 5.9% three months on

Every rate here is what a panel row can act on: the signal fired on all 9,417 client-weeks, scored against what happened the week after.

Every model-based approach either failed or restated what was already known. The per-client mood vector stayed flat through a real escalation. Ego state did not move. Clients at their angriest still read as Adult stance — they do not start shouting, they stay polite and get unhappy more often.

The loops that pay off are the dotted ones, and none of them needs a model. Counting verdicts per client per month finds an escalation three months before anyone notices. Counting messages and replies finds the clients about to escalate this week. The vector machinery in the solid path does neither.

How fast we answer

The number the product was built to move, over 12 months of client mail.

The firm is faster on angry mail, not slower. It answers 53% of complaints against 36% of ordinary mail, at a shorter median and a much shorter tail. "We went quiet and they got angry" is not what the data shows — reply latency in the run-up to a first complaint was 20.5h against a 19.6h baseline, and the answer rate was slightly better. The real gap is the 47% of complaints that get no reply at all, and the p90 of 124.9 hours: five days for the slowest tenth.

That is the engagement signal wearing a disguise. We reply when something is happening, and something happening is what predicts a complaint. A week where we answered nothing is mostly a week where nothing arrived.

Reply speed does not predict escalation. Across a twentyfold range of response time the rate moves from 15.3% to 18.7%, on 150 weeks in each tail bucket. The tail leans the right way; the counts cannot separate it from flat.

So the lever is which client, not how fast. Answering everyone quicker is expensive and buys no measurable reduction in escalation. Answering the right client — engaged, and complaining inside four weeks — starts from a 24.7% chance they escalate again, four times the base rate, and that is where a bookkeeper's morning should go. Speed still matters for reasons this corpus cannot see: satisfaction, renewal, what a client says to their peers. It just cannot be sold as escalation prevention.

Gemini decides live

The only paid step and the only one that decides anything. v1.9 learned that asking when about work we already hold is a complaint, after the prompt had explicitly taught the opposite.

05

Name the device, or say nothing live

Telling someone "this is a complaint" moves one email. Telling them "'not good to have so many iterations' is understatement, read it as 'this is bad'" moves every future email, including the ones we never flag.

Only patterns that name something literally written are shown. Ones that infer what the writer meant were wrong 4 times in 10 and stay silent.

Only a quarter of complaints use explicit failure wording. The rest arrive as "could you provide an update on the expected timeline": courteous, blameless, and understood by every American reader as a chase. That gap is the entire product.

The tone branches all died for one reason. Hotspot clients read 94% Adult stance even in their angriest months. They do not start shouting; they stay polite and get unhappy more often. The signal is content and frequency, never register.

What is worth building next

One measured result is not yet in the product, and one instrument beats the model on precision.

IdeaMeasuredState
Promote on a nearest labelled neighbour86% / 60% recallnot built
…the same, on a client with no history75% / 30% recallit is client memory, not detection
Clear mail on benign neighboursloses 6–17 of 20ruled out
Wire the embedding gate7 in 10 at 1-in-5 sentlibrary only

Nearest-neighbour lookup is more precise than the production model — 86% against 70% — but half of that comes from the client's own history. Excluding it drops the result to 75% precision and halves the recall. It is institutional memory rather than complaint detection: it recognises this client complaining again, which is legitimate where the history exists and worth far less on a new tenant.
