How a client's complaint reaches the person who needs to see it

A bookkeeper in Pune reads a hundred emails to find the three that matter, and the three rarely say they matter. This is the pipeline that finds them, what is running, and what turned out not to work.

What is running today

Read this before the design. Most of the design is built and switched off, and the honest version of that is a table rather than a diagram.

Every email that survives the spam filter goes to the model. There is no embedding gate in production, so the cost and latency figures below describe what it would buy, not what it saves.

The loop that pays off is the dotted one, and it needs no model. Counting verdicts per client per month finds an escalation three months before anyone notices; the vector machinery below does not.

What each stage buys

01

Skip a client's own customers live

We keep the books for operating businesses, and their customer-facing systems copy us on traffic that is nothing to do with our work. 42 homeowner replies about pool cleaning were labelled complaints: real, and none of them about us.

The results were already discarded downstream, but that ran after the model answered. Now the call is never made.

cuts the notification platform,
not the client
0 tokens spent on it
02

Embed on arrival deployed

768 numbers stored on the row. Ollama pinned to one version with the model baked in, because a different implementation returns 768 plausible numbers with no error and the gate quietly stops meaning what it was trained to mean.

03

The gate not wired in

Ordering and latency, not cost — reading every client email is about $7 a month, so there was never a cost case for dropping mail. Honest numbers, from a fit that never saw the test mail.

1 in 5 sent → 7 in 10 caught
2 in 5 sent → 9 in 10
7.5 KB of coefficients
04

Gemini decides live

The only paid step and the only one that decides anything. v1.9 learned that asking when about work we already hold is a complaint, after the prompt had explicitly taught the opposite.

This is now the binding constraint. Tightening the gate cannot recover a complaint the judge misreads, because the gate already sent it.

19 of 20 complaints caught
9 false alarms in 49
~$7/month, full coverage
05

Name the device, or say nothing live

Telling someone "this is a complaint" moves one email. Telling them "'not good to have so many iterations' is understatement, read it as 'this is bad'" moves every future email, including the ones we never flag.

Only patterns that name something literally written are shown. Ones that infer what the writer meant were wrong 4 times in 10 and stay silent.

14 of 14 correct, held out
fires on 1 in 10 complaints
silent on the rest
06

Show where the client is heading live

A count cannot separate a client getting worse from one who has always been difficult. Each fire row now reads Rising 5%→36% or Cooling 25%→9%.

The threshold is measured: under 10% behaves exactly like zero, and crossing it does not revert.

under 10% → 2.0% next month
over 10% → 7.9% next month
still 5.9% three months on

The numbers that matter

Measured against 49 emails a person read and judged, and against 1,015 real complaints. No public corpus matches this domain, so those 49 rows are the only human instrument.

3%of client mail is a complaint
5 of 20complaints that say so plainly
10%the rate a client never comes back from
5clients escalating right now

Only a quarter of complaints use explicit failure wording. The rest arrive as "could you provide an update on the expected timeline": courteous, blameless, and understood by every American reader as a chase. That gap is the entire product.

What did not work

Recorded because each was measured and closed, and because several are the obvious next idea.

ApproachResultWhy it failed
Bag of wordsPR-AUC 0.221beaten by embeddings at 1/500th the size
A hand-written idiom lexicon13% recall11 of 18 patterns never fired once; an invented register
Transactional analysis, ego states11× liftadds zero recall over sentiment
Politeness theoryp = 0.36B2B mail is uniformly polite
Synthetic training mail+0.6 ptsgain shrank as data grew sixfold
Retrieved examples in the prompt18 vs 19 of 20no better than the rulebook, at full pool density
A mood vector per clientflattopic dominates the embedding; stance does not survive averaging

The tone branches all died for one reason. Hotspot clients read 94% Adult stance even in their angriest months. They do not start shouting; they stay polite and get unhappy more often. The signal is content and frequency, never register.

What is worth building next

One measured result is not yet in the product, and one instrument beats the model on precision.

IdeaMeasuredState
Promote on a nearest labelled neighbour85% precision at k=5not built
…with same-client neighbours capped88% precisionnot built
Clear mail on benign neighboursloses 6–17 of 20ruled out
Wire the embedding gate7 in 10 at 1-in-5 sentlibrary only

Nearest-neighbour lookup is more precise than the production model — 85% against 70% — while catching about half as much, at no cost and in 80 milliseconds. It works because clients repeat themselves: a global boundary averages every client's private grammar into one, and there is no such average.
